Phase 2: the router

app/router.py — A→B routing that costs an edge by how well its destination
cell fits the rider, not by metres. 60 ms per route.

  build_graph / snap / route_a_to_b / route_summary, per the plan, plus
  calibrate_rider and compare_routes, which the plan did not anticipate
  needing and which turned out to be the whole job.

The hard gate (demand > skill + 2σ) removes the edge rather than pricing it,
because a safety gate you can buy past with a big enough detour budget is not
a gate. Every refusal is recorded with a reason for the UI.

Three things the plan got wrong about this data, all measured in
analysis/12_route_calibration.py (~18 s):

1. The stop-check "Send It = 1.5–1.8× direct" is unachievable. Max detour
   anywhere in the network is 1.137×. λ saturates (3, 6, 10, 20, 40, 80 give
   the identical route — cost = L(1+λ(1-flow)) → λL(1-flow)), and at 18 chars
   deleting the shortest path's edges DISCONNECTS A from B on every pair
   tested. The graph is a bundle of ride corridors, not a road network.

2. So routing runs on the 16-char grid, where disjoint second paths exist at
   1.35–1.38×. This reverses doc 13's "staying at CELL_CHARS=18" for routing
   only; scoring and the map are unchanged.

3. Skill and challenge were on different axes: rider_dna skill is peak lean
   per corner (30.2°), demand_p90 is a per-cell aggregate (median 7.4°).
   Subtracting them gave z ≈ -3 and flow 0 on all 21,130 cells.
   calibrate_rider measures the rider with the crowd's own ruler.

Two bugs in the Phase 1 output, fixed in the consumer:
  - median_metres is the boundary sample step (~21 m), not edge length; it
    understated a 42.8 km route as 10.1 km. Now centre-to-centre haversine.
  - 10_crowd_layer.py never read FS_CELL_CHARS (script 11 did), so a 16-char
    build silently produced an 18-char cell table.

The best number Phase 2 produced: gate = skill + 2σ, derived from the spread
of this rider's road choices, lands within 3% of the hardest road they have
actually ridden — at both cell sizes (1.025× and 0.992×), independently.

Honest caveat now in CONTEXT: across 200 O-D pairs the median demand gain is
−0.00°. The dial moves the route on a minority of pairs, so rehearsed start
points for the demo are a requirement, not stagecraft.
